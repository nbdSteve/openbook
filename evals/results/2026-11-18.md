## Running headers and footers

The rule is in `openbook_search::headers` and runs inside `chunk_pages_with`, so the ingestion worker and the evaluation use the same code (`docs/retrieval.md`, step 7). Measured on the committed pages, one document per section:

| Corpus | Running phrases found | Removals | Chunks with a running header in the heading path, before | after |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| college-algebra | 8 | 30 | 64 of 133 | 41 of 129 |
| american-government | 8 | 58 | 0 of 84 | 0 of 82 |

In college-algebra the pages are Markdown from page transcription, and the header is a line (`# SECTION 1.2 EXPONENTS ...`). In american-government the pages come from the PDF text layer (no page needed transcription), and the header and the footer are phrases inside one long paragraph (`... 40 Chapter 2 | The Constitution and Its Origins This OpenStax book is available for free at ...`). A rule that works on lines would have found nothing in this corpus, so the rule works on words. The 41 chunks that still have a running header in college-algebra carry the kept first occurrence (for example `CHAPTER 1 PREREQUISITES`, first seen on page 2). In american-government the first occurrence is at the bottom of its page, so it is removed too, and no chunk has a header.

### Variant: keep the first occurrence, or drop every one

A second variant drops every occurrence of a running phrase, because the first occurrence of `CHAPTER 1 PREREQUISITES` is not a real heading. Both variants run on the live caches (the cache holds the chunk texts of both):

| american-government, keep-first | 0.9574 | 0.9787 | 0.6534 | 0.8936 | 0.9178 | 0.0000 |
| american-government, drop-all | 0.9574 | 0.9787 | 0.6534 | 0.8936 | 0.9178 | 0.0000 |

The rule says: change only when the pooled Recall@8 or nDCG@8 improves without hurting gate accuracy or the false-gate rate on either corpus; a tie goes to the simpler variant (drop-all). The pooled Recall@8 is the same (0.9837) and the pooled nDCG@8 differs by 0.0001 (0.6078 against 0.6079), which is a tie. But drop-all lowers the gate accuracy of college-algebra (0.8879 to 0.8785, one question more is missed) and the pooled gate accuracy (0.9000 to 0.8944). It hurts gate accuracy, so it does not qualify. **keep-first stays.** The data rests on one gate question (1 of 107), which is thin, so the reason that counts is the other one: keep-first keeps one copy of a phrase that may be a real heading, so it is the safer choice when the data cannot tell the two apart. The ranking does not care about the bogus heading, and the gate accuracy is slightly better with it. The drop-all code stays only for the grid (`--header-variant drop-all`); nothing in the ingestion worker or the API calls it.

The limit 0.78 still gives no false gate on the prose corpus. Its worst covered question is `paraphrase-13` ("Which powers stay with the states because the national government was not given them?") at 0.7581, so the margin is 0.0219, just above the 0.02 that the rule asks for. The limit gates 8 of 14 `not_covered` questions (57 percent; college-algebra: 7 of 19, 37 percent) and all 12 `off_topic` questions (0.8718 to 0.9486). The `not_covered` topics of the prose corpus that get through (6 of 14) lie at 0.61 to 0.78, the same band as the covered ones, so a threshold cannot separate them, as in round 1.

Over both corpora, 0.78 is the lowest limit that passes the margin rule (0.77 has a margin of 0.0119 and 0.76 of 0.0019, both below 0.02). For college-algebra alone, 0.76 would pass and have a higher accuracy (0.9159), but the pooled worst covered question comes from the prose corpus, so 0.78 stays. The distance limit is the same for both subjects, which is the point of the second corpus: the limit did not need to change for prose.

## Choices

The rule: change a default only when it improves the pooled Recall@8 or nDCG@8 without hurting gate accuracy or the false-gate rate on either corpus. Ties go to the current default.

- **BM25 weight: unchanged (1.0).** Weight 0.5 has the best pooled nDCG@8 (0.6192 against 0.6078) and MRR (0.9000 against 0.8767), with the same Recall@8 and the same gate. But it breaks the property of round 1: with k 60 and 40 dense candidates a weight below about 0.9 means a chunk that only BM25 finds can never reach the sources, so BM25 could only re-order dense results. That needs strong evidence, and this is not: the Recall@8 does not move, the MRR gain is an effect of one corpus (college-algebra 0.8662 to 0.9105; american-government goes down, 0.8936 to 0.8830), and it is smaller than in round 1 (MRR +0.049 then, +0.023 now), so about half of the round 1 gain came from the running headers in the BM25 heading field, as round 1 suspected. Weight 0.75 (also below 0.9) gains 0.004 nDCG@8, which is noise. Weight 1.5 gives Recall@8 0.9919 (one question) and loses nDCG@8 (0.5993) and MRR (0.8437). A note about the property is in `docs/retrieval.md` and on the constant.
- **Header variant: keep-first** (see above). **Gate limit: 0.78**, unchanged (see above).
- **RRF k 30** has Recall@8 0.9919 against 0.9837 (one question of american-government: 0.9787 to 1.0000) and nDCG@8 +0.0024, with no change in the gates. It passes the rule on paper, but one question and 0.002 are noise (round 1 saw the same and called it noise), and k 60 is the usual value. Unchanged.
- **Chunk target 600 tokens** has pooled Recall@8 0.9919 (+0.008), nDCG@8 0.6300 (+0.022), and a higher gate accuracy (0.9056). It also passes the rule on paper. I did not change it, for four reasons. (1) The gain is all in the prose corpus (nDCG@8 0.6534 to 0.7149, gate accuracy 0.9178 to 0.9315); college-algebra is a little worse (nDCG@8 0.5796 to 0.5774, MRR 0.8662 to 0.8605). (2) 600 tokens is the hard limit of the chunker (`MAX_CHARS` 2400), so a target of 600 leaves no room to pack and breaks the "about 400, at most 600" design. (3) The gate limit was calibrated on 400-token chunks, and a bigger chunk shifts all distances. (4) A change means re-embedding every course. Two corpora of about 40 pages each do not carry that. Worth a test on a larger and more varied corpus.
- **Cap 6, final sources 8, candidates 40: unchanged.** Cap 8 (no cap) has nDCG@8 0.6298 against 0.6078, and the same reasoning as round 1 holds: the cap costs a little here and protects source diversity in a real course, which these sets do not measure. 20 candidates gives nDCG@8 0.6100 (+0.002), noise, and the corpora are small.

## What the misses have in common

Two hybrid misses at Recall@8: `college-algebra/paraphrase-19` (the quadratic formula, the same miss as in round 1) and `american-government/followup-03` ("In which year did the Supreme Court first claim that power?", after "What is judicial review?"; the rewrite fetched pages about McCulloch v. Maryland, which also talk about the Supreme Court and its powers, and page 17 with Marbury v. Madison was not in the eight). Both are ranking problems inside one neighborhood, not data problems.

## Notes

- The prose corpus was labeled by hand against the page text. Every `not_covered` topic was searched for in the excerpt text first (Federalist Papers, First Amendment, Electoral College, filibuster, Speaker of the House, political action committees, gerrymandering, Brown v. Board, Miranda, Fourth Amendment, Missouri Compromise, executive orders, state vehicle emission standards, Roe v. Wade). The key terms and the summary of chapter 3 name the topics of sections 3.3 to 3.5, which the excerpt does not hold, so no `not_covered` question uses them.
- The corpus PDF was shrunk from 7.9 MB to 1.6 MB with Ghostscript. The text that pdfium reads is byte for byte the same (the dev cache pages and the embedding keys did not change).
- The cache is 740 KB for american-government and 852 KB for college-algebra.
- The distance limit is valid for Titan v2 only.
