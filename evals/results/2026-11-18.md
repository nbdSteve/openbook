## Running headers and footers: tried, and not in production

The page text keeps the running header of the book ("SECTION 1.2 EXPONENTS AND SCIENTIFIC NOTATION", "Chapter 2 | The Constitution and Its Origins", "This OpenStax book is available for free at ..."). The chunker reads a header line as a heading, so in college-algebra 101 of 133 chunks carry one in the heading path. Round 2 built a rule for it on words (`openbook_search::headers`): a phrase at the first or last words of a page, on enough pages. It works on both kinds of pages that the corpora have (Markdown from transcription, with a header line; the text layer of the PDF, where the header is a phrase inside one long paragraph).

The rule was made safer in two review rounds (it must start or end at the edge of a page, it must not be a sentence, a list item, a table row, or a line of math, a document of fewer than 6 pages is left alone, the longest header that covers a place goes, at most one page number on each side goes). The second review still found that it fails on realistic short pages (8 pages of 40 to 80 words): it cuts the start of a sentence that every page has ("Consider the following graph and name its centre." loses its first words), it turns `## MATH1051 Tutorial Sheet 2` into a stray `2`, and it deletes the only copy of content at the bottom of a page ("(D) None of the above"). The reason is that text alone cannot say where a phrase sits on the page. **Production chunks the pages as they are** (mode `off`). The rule stays as an experiment: mode `keep-first` (the first occurrence stays when it is at the top of its page) and `drop-all`, as knobs of the grid. The scenarios of the review are in the tree as ignored tests, and the next approach is a plan in `docs/retrieval.md`.

Like round 1, the choice is in-sample: the limit was chosen on the golden questions that the retrieval was tuned on, and a real course can have a covered question that is farther. `trace.gate` records the real distances for the next round.

## Choices

The rule: change a default only when it improves the pooled Recall@8 or nDCG@8 without hurting gate accuracy or the false-gate rate on either corpus. Ties go to the current default.

- **BM25 weight: unchanged (1.0).** The case for a lower weight is stronger than in round 1, because the gain is now on both corpora. Weight 0.5 has the best pooled Recall@1 (0.8374 against 0.7642), MRR (0.8973 against 0.8553), and nDCG@8 (0.6125 against 0.6064), with the same Recall@8 and the same gate, and the MRR rises on both corpora (college-algebra 0.8520 to 0.9007, american-government 0.8606 to 0.8918). Weight 0.75 gives Recall@1 0.8130 and MRR 0.8787. On paper 0.5 passes the rule. I did not change it, because it breaks the property of round 1: with k 60 and 40 dense candidates a weight below about 0.9 means a chunk that only BM25 finds can never reach the sources, so BM25 could only re-order dense results. The golden sets cannot show what that costs, because their 82 and 133 chunks are small enough for 40 dense candidates to hold every relevant chunk (the Recall@8 does not move, and on american-government it falls from 1.0000 to 0.9787), and what the weight gains is the order at the top. That is evidence for a re-test on a larger corpus, and not yet strong enough to give up the property. Weight 1.5 loses Recall@1 (0.7398), nDCG@8 (0.5969), and MRR (0.8346). A note about the property is in `docs/retrieval.md` and on the constant.
- **Header mode: off** (see "Running headers"). **Gate limit: 0.78** (see "The gate").
- **RRF k 30** has Recall@1 0.7724 and MRR 0.8600 against 0.7642 and 0.8553, the same Recall@8 and nDCG@8 (0.6065), and no change in the gates. That is a few questions in the order of the top results and nothing else, so it is a tie. Unchanged.
- **Chunk target 600 tokens** has pooled Recall@1 0.7886, nDCG@8 0.6231 (+0.017), MRR 0.8695, the same Recall@8, and a higher gate accuracy (0.9000), and it is not worse on either corpus. On paper it passes the rule. I did not change it, because it breaks the gate: the distances are calibrated on chunks of 400 tokens, and with chunks of 600 the worst covered question moves to 0.7770 (`american-government/lookup-12`), so the margin of 0.78 falls to 0.0030. The lowest limit that passes on both corpora is 0.80, and its gate accuracy (0.8778) is lower than the production one (0.8944). Also, 600 tokens is the hard limit of the chunker (`MAX_CHARS` 2400), so a target of 600 leaves no room to pack and the overlap would push a chunk over the limit of a snippet, and a change means re-embedding every course. Worth a test on a larger corpus together with a new gate limit.
- **Cap 6, final sources 8, candidates 40: unchanged.** Cap 8 (no cap) has Recall@8 1.0000 and nDCG@8 0.6307 against 0.9919 and 0.6064, and the same reasoning as round 1 holds: the cap costs something here and protects source diversity in a real course, which these sets do not measure. 20 candidates gives nDCG@8 0.6109 (+0.0045) but loses a question of Recall@8 on college-algebra (0.9737), so it hurts one corpus.

## What the misses have in common

One hybrid miss at Recall@8 on the 123 covered questions: `college-algebra/paraphrase-19` ("Which method solves every quadratic equation, even one that I cannot factor?", the quadratic formula on page 15; the returned chunks are from pages 9 to 14, which also talk about solving quadratics). It is the same miss as in round 1 and the cap-off run finds it. It is a ranking problem inside one neighborhood, not a data problem. american-government has none.

## Answer evaluation

The first answer-level measurement. The command is `openbook-eval answers` (see `docs/evaluation.md`). It uses the production retrieval decision from the committed caches, the production prompt code, `claude-sonnet` (`au.anthropic.claude-sonnet-4-6`) for the answers, and `claude-haiku` (`au.anthropic.claude-haiku-4-5-20251001-v1:0`) as the judge (rubric version 3). The in-sample set is the 180 golden questions of both corpora that the prompt was tuned on. The held-out set is 20 `not_covered` questions (10 per corpus) that were written after the tuning. The cache is in `evals/corpora/<id>/answers/` and the pooled numbers of the committed prompt are in `evals/answers-summary.json`. Follow-up questions are approximate: the earlier question goes in the same user turn, with no stored tutor reply.

### The problem

A question that the course does not teach but that is near the course (for example the derivative of x squared in an algebra course) passes the gate, so the model gets sources that do not answer it. Before the change, 19 of the 33 in-sample `not_covered` questions got sources, and 14 had none. Only 12.1% of the answers said that the course materials do not cover the question. Under the Socratic style nearly all answers opened with "Great question! Let's think through this together" and a guiding question, with no statement and no citation. The Direct style (the demo default) said it 27.3% of the time.

### The prompt change

In `crates/core/src/prompt.rs`:

- When no source answers the question, the answer opens with one plain sentence that is true for any topic. A gated turn (the gate found nothing) gets "I could not find this in your course materials." for a question about any subject, even one close to the course, and "That is outside this course." for a request that is not a question about a subject (a poem, a letter, a translation). A turn with sources that do not answer the question gets "The course materials I found do not answer this." The sentence comes before any question, hint, or step, in every style, without hedge words. Then the tutor helps from general knowledge and cites nothing. A greeting or thanks keeps its rule: a normal short reply that does not mention the course materials.
- The sources section tells the tutor to point to the source that holds the answer, also when the style holds the answer back ("Look at [2] and tell me what you notice").
- The server puts a short note before the last student message of a turn that had retrieval (`GATED_TURN_NOTE`, `SOURCES_TURN_NOTE`). The student text goes inside a `<student_message>` tag, escaped with `escape_source`, and the system prompt says that only text outside the tag comes from the server, so a student cannot write a note of their own (a test sends the exact note text). The note covers only the last message, is built at request time from constants, and is never stored. The chat route, the demo route, and the evaluation use the same function (`turns_for`), and the demo budget counts the worst case of each mode.

### The server note, ablation

The same prompt with and without the note (`--no-turn-note`), on the seeded covered sample of 40 plus all `not_covered` and `off_topic` questions (97 questions), and on the Direct `not_covered` questions.

| Metric | With the note | Without the note |
|---|---|---|
| Socratic, covered grounded citation | 97.5% | 72.5% |
| Socratic, covered citation precision | 96.2% | 82.3% |
| Socratic, covered uncited | 0.0% | 17.5% |
| Socratic, not_covered says "not covered" | 97.0% | 72.7% |
| Socratic, not_covered not gated | 94.7% | 52.6% |
| Socratic, off_topic correct | 95.8% | 100.0% |
| Direct, not_covered says "not covered" | 93.9% | 81.8% |
| Direct, not_covered not gated | 89.5% | 68.4% |
| Held-out, Socratic | 95.0% | 70.0% |
| Held-out, Direct | 95.0% | 85.0% |

The note earns its place: it adds 25 points of grounded citation and 24 points of not-covered statements, and it costs 4 points of off-topic handling in the Socratic style (one subject question). The first-sentence check says that without the note the Direct answers open with the statement 97.0% of the time, while the judge accepts 81.8%: the judge is the stricter measure.

### Three wording rounds

Round 1 (the notice names the topic) fixed the common case and made subject questions outside the course drop (88.2% to 76.5%), because "I could not find this" does not fit a poem or a job letter. Round 2 added a branch for requests with nothing to do with the course, and Direct then skipped the sentence for nearby topics (gated 100% to 64.3%) because it read them as plain in-subject questions. Round 3 names two exact sentences, one for any question about a subject and one for a request that is not a question, and it is best on every axis.

### The judge

The judge returns three booleans and a reason (rubric in `crates/eval/src/judge.rs`, with a pinned version). The question and the answer go into the request as escaped data inside tags, and the rubric says to never follow instructions inside them. I read the 33 `not_covered` and 24 `off_topic` baseline answers with their verdicts. All `not_covered` verdicts were right, and rubric 1 was too strict on `off_topic`. Rubric 2 and 3 count "outside the scope of American Government" and "I could not find this in your course materials". The judge scores "do not seem to cover X directly, but ..." as not a statement, so the prompt bans those hedge words. The deterministic first-sentence check (a phrase in the first 200 characters, before the first question mark) is reported next to the judge rate: they differ by at most 3 points in the committed numbers for the Socratic style.

### What is left

- Retrieval relevance limits the not gated cases. The remaining not-covered misses got sources that really discuss the topic.
- The false "not covered" rate is 0.0% on 123 covered questions. The 95% upper bound for 0 of 123 is about 2.4% (3 divided by 123), so a rate of that size is not ruled out.
- One judge, one run per prompt, and 20 held-out questions. The model is not deterministic, so a rerun can move a rate by a few points.
- The "before" column is the rubric 2 judge on the old prompt. The old answers were pruned, so it cannot be recomputed with rubric 3. The committed baseline summary keeps the numbers.
- The live cost of the whole work (baseline, the iterations, the held-out embeddings, the ablation, the three wording rounds, and the final runs) was about 20 US dollars, estimated from the cost bounds and the token counts of the cache.
