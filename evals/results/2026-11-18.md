## Running headers and footers

The rule is in `openbook_search::headers` and runs inside `chunk_pages_with`, so the ingestion worker and the evaluation use the same code (`docs/retrieval.md`, step 7). The first version of this rule, which the first refresh used, removed real content from some course material (a bullet on every step of a slide, a task of a worksheet, a table that goes over pages, numbers in a text). The review found it, and the rule is now built to remove too little: a phrase must start at the first word or end at the last word of a page, it must not be a sentence, a list item, a table row, or a line of math, a document of fewer than 6 pages is left alone, the pass runs twice, the longest header that covers a place is removed, and at most one page number on each side of a header goes, only when it fits the page numbers of the document. Each of these cases is a unit test. Measured on the committed pages, one document per section:

| Corpus | Running phrases found | Removals | Chunks with a running header in the heading path, before | after | Chunks with a fragment of a running header, before | after |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| college-algebra | 8 | 25 | 101 of 133 | 62 of 126 | 0 | 0 |
| american-government | 8 | 37 | 0 of 84 | 0 of 82 | 0 | 0 |

In college-algebra the pages are Markdown from page transcription, and the header is a line (`# SECTION 1.2 EXPONENTS ...`). In american-government the pages come from the PDF text layer (no page needed transcription), and the header and the footer are phrases inside one long paragraph (`... 40 Chapter 2 | The Constitution and Its Origins This OpenStax book is available for free at ...`). A rule that works on lines would have found nothing in this corpus, so the rule works on words. The stricter rule removes less than the first version in college-algebra (25 removals, not 30): on a page that is short, or that is one long line from the text layer, a title that is longer than a fifth of the page is not removed, which is the price of never cutting a phrase out of a sentence. 62 of 126 chunks of college-algebra still have a running header in the heading path: the first occurrence of each phrase stays (`CHAPTER 1 PREREQUISITES`, first seen on page 2, heads pages 2 to 8), and a title that the rule does not remove stays too. No chunk has a fragment (a heading that is a part of a longer header, such as `SECTION` from `SECTION 5.1 QUADRATIC FUNCTIONS`). In american-government the first occurrence is at the bottom of its page, so it is removed too, and no chunk has a header.

## The gate

Rule of the choice: no false gate on the golden questions, a margin of at least 0.02 between the limit and the worst covered question (a safety condition, and it comes first), then the highest gate accuracy among the limits that pass. Titan v2, the best dense distance at most the limit.

|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| american-government | 47 | 0.78 | 0 | 0.7581 | 0.0219 | 8 of 14 | 12 of 12 | 0.9178 |
| american-government | 47 | 0.79 | 0 | 0.7581 | 0.0319 | 6 of 14 | 12 of 12 | 0.8904 |
| college-algebra | 76 | 0.78 | 0 | 0.7640 | 0.0160 | 7 of 19 | 12 of 12 | 0.8879 |
| college-algebra | 76 | 0.79 | 0 | 0.7640 | 0.0260 | 4 of 19 | 12 of 12 | 0.8598 |
| combined | 123 | 0.78 | 0 | 0.7640 | 0.0160 | 15 of 33 | 24 of 24 | 0.9000 |
| combined | 123 | 0.79 | 0 | 0.7640 | 0.0260 | 10 of 33 | 24 of 24 | 0.8722 |

**Decision: the limit moves from 0.78 to 0.79.** Round 1 chose 0.78 on one corpus, with a margin of 0.032. On the prose corpus alone 0.78 still passes (the worst covered question is `paraphrase-13` at 0.7581, margin 0.0219). But the stricter header rule moved the worst covered question of college-algebra, `paraphrase-12`, from 0.7392 to 0.7640, because more chunks keep a running header in their heading and embed farther from the question. At 0.78 the margin is then 0.0160 on college-algebra and 0.0160 pooled, under the 0.02 that the rule asks for. The lowest limit that passes on both corpora is 0.79 (margin 0.0260 on college-algebra, 0.0319 on american-government, 0.0260 pooled). The margin is a safety condition and it comes before the accuracy: a false gate (a student with a real course question gets no sources) costs more than a missed gate (the question gets sources that the answer may not cite, and the UI shows only the cited ones). So 0.79 is the limit, even though it costs gate accuracy.

What 0.79 costs and keeps: the false-gate rate is 0 on both corpora. All 12 `off_topic` questions of each corpus are still gated (the nearest is 0.8232 in college-algebra and 0.8789 in american-government), so the off-topic catch stays at 100 percent. Five more `not_covered` questions get through than at 0.78 (the share caught falls from 0.4545 to 0.3030 pooled; american-government 8 of 14 to 6 of 14, college-algebra 7 of 19 to 4 of 19), and the pooled gate accuracy falls from 0.9000 to 0.8722. The `not_covered` topics that get through lie in the same band as the covered questions, so a threshold cannot separate them, as in round 1. Like round 1, this is in-sample: the limit was chosen on the golden questions that the retrieval was tuned on, and a real course can have a covered question that is farther. `trace.gate` records the real distances for the next round.

## Choices

The rule: change a default only when it improves the pooled Recall@8 or nDCG@8 without hurting gate accuracy or the false-gate rate on either corpus. Ties go to the current default.

- **BM25 weight: unchanged (1.0).** Weight 0.5 has the best pooled nDCG@8 (0.6221 against 0.6129) and MRR (0.8980 against 0.8806), with the same Recall@8 and the same gate. But it breaks the property of round 1: with k 60 and 40 dense candidates a weight below about 0.9 means a chunk that only BM25 finds can never reach the sources, so BM25 could only re-order dense results. That needs strong evidence, and this is not: the Recall@8 does not move, the MRR gain is an effect of one corpus (college-algebra 0.8726 to 0.9072; american-government goes down, 0.8936 to 0.8830), and it is smaller than in round 1 (MRR +0.049 then, +0.017 now), so a good part of the round 1 gain came from the running headers in the BM25 heading field, as round 1 suspected. Weight 0.75 (also below 0.9) gains 0.0053 nDCG@8. Weight 1.5 gives Recall@8 0.9919 (two questions) and loses nDCG@8 (0.6005) and MRR (0.8474). A note about the property is in `docs/retrieval.md` and on the constant.
- **Header variant: keep-first** (see above). **Gate limit: 0.79**, changed from 0.78 by the margin rule (see "The gate").
- **RRF k 30** has Recall@8 0.9919 against 0.9756 (one question on each corpus) and nDCG@8 +0.0013, with no change in the gates. It passes the rule on paper, but two questions and 0.001 are noise (round 1 saw the same and called it noise), and k 60 is the usual value. Unchanged.
- **Chunk target 600 tokens** has pooled Recall@8 0.9919, nDCG@8 0.6381 (+0.025), MRR 0.8897, and a higher gate accuracy (0.8833), and it is not worse on either corpus now. On paper it passes the rule. I did not change it, because it breaks the gate: the distances are calibrated on chunks of 400 tokens, and with chunks of 600 the worst covered question of american-government moves to 0.7730 (`lookup-12`), so the margin of 0.79 falls to 0.0170 pooled and 0.0170 on american-government. The lowest limit that passes on both corpora is 0.80, and its gate accuracy (0.8722) is lower than the production one (0.8722). Also, 600 tokens is the hard limit of the chunker (`MAX_CHARS` 2400), so a target of 600 leaves no room to pack and the overlap would push a chunk over the limit of a snippet, and a change means re-embedding every course. Worth a test on a larger corpus together with a new gate limit.
- **Cap 6, final sources 8, candidates 40: unchanged.** Cap 8 (no cap) has nDCG@8 0.6394 against 0.6129 and Recall@8 0.9837, and the same reasoning as round 1 holds: the cap costs a little here and protects source diversity in a real course, which these sets do not measure. 20 candidates gives nDCG@8 0.6165 (+0.0036), noise, and the corpora are small.

## What the misses have in common

Three hybrid misses at Recall@8: `college-algebra/paraphrase-19` (the quadratic formula, the same miss as in round 1), `college-algebra/paraphrase-13` ("How do I find the point where a parabola turns around from its formula?": the chunks of pages 27 to 29 and 36 come first, and the chunk of pages 31 and 32 is out of the eight), and `american-government/followup-03` ("In which year did the Supreme Court first claim that power?", after "What is judicial review?": the rewrite fetched pages about McCulloch v. Maryland, which also talk about the Supreme Court and its powers, and page 17 with Marbury v. Madison was not in the eight). All three are ranking problems inside one neighborhood, not data problems.
