Data: 107 golden questions (76 covered, 19 `not_covered`, 12 `off_topic`), 133 chunks of 400 tokens in five sections, embedding model Titan Text Embeddings v2, the live cache `evals/cache`. Everything here is offline and deterministic: `openbook-eval offline` and `openbook-eval grid`.

Cap-off mode (the per-document cap never skips a chunk): Recall@5 0.9605, Recall@8 1.0000, nDCG@8 0.6076, MRR 0.8536.

Changes of production defaults:
- **Gate**: Titan v2 uses the dense distance only, limit 0.78 (was: distance 0.85 or any BM25 hit).
- **Most sources per document**: 6 (was 4). The cap is soft now.
- Nothing else changed: RRF k, final sources, candidates, BM25 weight (1.0), and chunk target stay.

## The gate

Rule: a question has sources when its best dense distance is at most 0.78. The fake embedder and unknown models keep the first rule, because their distances have another scale.

What the gate catches (hybrid, 0.78):

| Group | Questions | Gated | Missed-gate rate |
|---|---|---|---|
| covered | 76 | 0 (false-gate rate 0) | - |
| not_covered | 19 | 6 | 0.6842 |
| off_topic | 12 | 12 | 0.0000 |
| both | 31 | 18 | 0.4194 |

Adjacent math topics are hard to gate on retrieval scores. The best dense distance of a covered question is at most 0.748 (paraphrase-12). The `not_covered` questions are 0.559 to 0.863, and 13 of 19 lie below 0.78. Off-topic questions are 0.82 to 0.94 and the gate catches all of them, including "hi", "thanks, that makes sense", and "ok cool". The gate cannot tell "nearby math that the course does not teach" from "a paraphrase of what the course teaches", and a tighter limit would cut covered questions. A better answer for the adjacent topics needs the model to judge, not a threshold. Small talk is gated too ("hi", "thanks, that makes sense", "ok cool"), so the not-covered notice of the prompt now tells the model to answer small talk normally and to say "the materials do not seem to cover it" only for a subject question.

Choice by the decision rule (no false gate, a margin of at least 0.02 beyond the worst covered question, then the highest gate accuracy, then the simpler rule):
- Rule (a), dense only: the limits 0.77 to 0.82 pass. 0.77 has gate accuracy 0.8879 (margin 0.022), 0.78 has 0.8785 (margin 0.032). They differ by one question, so the larger margin wins: 0.78.
- Rule (b), dense or a strong lexical match: the best (b) rows tie with (a) at 0.78 (minimum 4 matched terms) or are worse. The lexical part rescues no covered question that (a) loses, and it lets some `not_covered` questions through. So (a), the simpler rule, wins.
- The first rule (any BM25 hit) has no false gate but catches almost nothing.

"No false gate" is in-sample: it holds on the 76 covered golden questions, with the limit 0.032 above the worst one (0.748). A real course can have a covered question that is farther. `trace.gate` (best dense distance, top BM25 score, term coverage, rule name) is stored with every turn, so the next round can measure real distances. A turn that needs a rewrite (a follow-up) and whose rewrite failed uses the first rule (any BM25 hit passes) and sets `trace.rewriteFailed`, because the raw message is far from every chunk. An embedding model that is not Titan v2 gets the same permissive rule, logs a warning at API start, and the trace names the rule.

Where the margin comes from: one question, paraphrase-12, has the worst covered distance. A different golden set could move the limit. Re-run the sweep when the golden set or the embedding model changes.

### Chosen rows

- Most sources per document: 6, a product choice. Measured from the old setting (cap 4): Recall@8 0.9605 to 0.9868 and nDCG@8 0.5151 to 0.5758, with the same gate accuracy. A smaller cap is worse (cap 2: Recall@8 0.8947). Cap 8 (off) is better still on this set (Recall@8 1.0000, nDCG@8 0.6076), so cap 6 costs 0.032 nDCG@8 and one question of Recall@8 here. The cap exists for courses with several documents, so that one document cannot take all the places. This evaluation does not measure source diversity (how many documents the sources come from), so it cannot show the benefit of the cap, only its cost. I kept 6 and did not pick the in-sample best.
- BM25 weight: unchanged (1.0). A weight of 0.5 was better in-sample (MRR 0.8520 to 0.9007, Recall@8 0.9868 to 1.0000, nDCG@8 0.5758 to 0.5843) and I tried it first, but it is rejected because of a property of the fusion. With k 60 and 40 dense candidates, the best score of a chunk that only BM25 finds is 0.5 / 61 = 0.0082, and the worst score of a chunk that only dense search finds is 1 / 100 = 0.0100. So at weight 0.5, BM25 can only re-order the dense results. It can never add a source. That removes the main reason for hybrid search in a large course: an exact match for notation or a keyword that dense ranks low. The golden set hides this: 40 candidates are 30 percent of its 133 chunks (a run with only 10 dense candidates gives the same MRR). Part of the MRR gain may also come from the running headers in the BM25 heading field (see below). A unit test pins the property at the production numbers (a chunk at BM25 rank 1 that dense does not find reaches the final sources). Re-tune the weight after the headers are stripped and with a larger corpus.
- RRF k: unchanged (60). k 30 gives nDCG@8 0.5769 against 0.5758, and Recall does not move. That is noise.
- Final sources: unchanged (8). 6 loses nDCG@8 (0.5714) and, with the cap off, one question of Recall@8. 10 changes nothing.
- Candidates: unchanged (40). 20 gives nDCG@8 0.5828 but loses one question of Recall@8 (0.9737). The corpus has only 133 chunks, so 40 candidates is 30 percent of it. A real course has many more chunks. It needs a bigger corpus before it is trusted.
- Chunk target: unchanged (400). 600 tokens gives nDCG@8 0.5781 and Recall@5 0.9737 against 0.5758 and 0.9605, with a lower MRR (0.8491). The gain is within noise, and 300 tokens loses recall (0.9737). A change of the chunk target also means re-embedding every course, so it needs more than 0.01.

With the weight at 1.0 and the new metric, no choice changed from the first read of the grid except the BM25 weight, which is reverted.

## Running headers and footers (measured, not fixed)

The page Markdown keeps the running page header of the book ("SECTION 1.2 EXPONENTS AND SCIENTIFIC NOTATION", "CHAPTER 1 PREREQUISITES", "SECTION 2.5 QUADRATIC EQUATIONS", "SECTION 5.1 QUADRATIC FUNCTIONS"). The chunker reads each of them as a top-level heading, so it goes into the heading path of the chunks that follow it, into the text that Titan embeds, and into the BM25 heading field. 76 of the 133 chunks have a running header in their heading path. Example: chunk `exponents:0` (page 1) has the heading path "SECTION 1.2 EXPONENTS AND SCIENTIFIC NOTATION > LEARNING OBJECTIVES". The header repeats on pages 1, 3, 5, and 7 of that section. A question about scientific notation matches these headers (the BM25 hit of not-covered-17 comes from these headers, and the course does not teach the topic). Stripping them is for the next round.
