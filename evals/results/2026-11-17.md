Data: 107 golden questions (76 covered, 19 `not_covered`, 12 `off_topic`), 133 chunks of 400 tokens in five sections, embedding model Titan Text Embeddings v2, the live cache `evals/cache`. Everything here is offline and deterministic: `openbook-eval offline` and `openbook-eval grid`.

On the same 107 questions, the first gate rule (any BM25 hit passes, distance 0.85) has gate accuracy 0.7477 and misses 100 percent of `not_covered` and 66.7 percent of `off_topic`.

Changes of production defaults:
- **Gate**: Titan v2 uses the dense distance only, limit 0.78 (was: distance 0.85 or any BM25 hit).
- **Most sources per document**: 6 (was 4). The cap is soft now.
- **BM25 weight in the fusion**: 0.5 (was 1.0).
- Nothing else changed: RRF k, final sources, candidates, and chunk target stay.

## The gate

Rule: a question has sources when its best dense distance is at most 0.78. The fake embedder and unknown models keep the first rule, because their distances have another scale.

What the gate catches (hybrid, 0.78):

| Group | Questions | Gated | Missed-gate rate |
|---|---|---|---|
| covered | 76 | 0 (false-gate rate 0) | - |
| not_covered | 19 | 6 | 0.6842 |
| off_topic | 12 | 12 | 0.0000 |
| both | 31 | 18 | 0.4194 |

Adjacent math topics are hard to gate on retrieval scores. The best dense distance of a covered question is at most 0.748 (paraphrase-12). The `not_covered` questions are 0.559 to 0.863, and 13 of 19 lie below 0.78. Off-topic questions are 0.82 to 0.94 and the gate catches all of them, including "hi", "thanks, that makes sense", and "ok cool". The gate cannot tell "nearby math that the course does not teach" from "a paraphrase of what the course teaches", and a tighter limit would cut covered questions. A better answer for the adjacent topics needs the model to judge, not a threshold.

Choice by the decision rule (no false gate, a margin of at least 0.02 beyond the worst covered question, then the highest gate accuracy, then the simpler rule):
- Rule (a), dense only: the limits 0.77 to 0.82 pass. 0.77 has gate accuracy 0.8879 (margin 0.022), 0.78 has 0.8785 (margin 0.032). They differ by one question, so the larger margin wins: 0.78.
- Rule (b), dense or a strong lexical match: the best (b) rows tie with (a) at 0.78 (minimum 4 matched terms) or are worse. The lexical part rescues no covered question that (a) loses, and it lets some `not_covered` questions through. So (a), the simpler rule, wins.
- The first rule (any BM25 hit) has no false gate but catches almost nothing.

Where the margin comes from: one question, paraphrase-12, has the worst covered distance. A different golden set could move the limit. Re-run the sweep when the golden set or the embedding model changes.

### Chosen rows

- Most sources per document: 6. Measured from the old setting (cap 4, BM25 weight 1): Recall@8 0.9605 to 0.9868 and nDCG@10 0.4936 to 0.5526, with the same gate accuracy. A smaller cap is worse (cap 2: Recall@8 0.8947). In the single-document mode the cap does not matter.
- BM25 weight: 0.5. From the cap 6 setting: Recall@8 0.9868 to 1.0000, nDCG@10 0.5526 to 0.5600, MRR 0.8520 to 0.9007, in the sectioned and the single-document mode alike. The dense list alone has MRR 0.8893, so the BM25 list was pulling good dense results down. The Recall@8 gain is one question, so the MRR gain (consistent in every row of the grid, with every cap) is the stronger evidence. At cap 4 alone, weight 0.5 would have lost one question of Recall@8 (0.9474), which is why the order of the two changes matters.
- RRF k: unchanged (60). k 30 gives nDCG@10 0.5636 against 0.5600, and Recall and MRR do not move. That is noise.
- Final sources: unchanged (8). Nothing changes between 6, 8, and 10 on this set (the golden set has one relevant chunk group per question).
- Candidates: unchanged (40). 20 gives nDCG@10 0.5767 but loses one question of Recall@8 (0.9868). The corpus has only 133 chunks, so 40 candidates is 30 percent of it. A real course has many more chunks. It needs a bigger corpus before it is trusted.

## What the misses have in common

The hybrid run has no miss at Recall@8 now. The three misses of the first baseline were these:
- paraphrase-13 and followup-11: the right pages were among the candidates, but the cap of 4 cut them out of the section that had many good chunks. The cap of 6 fixes both.
- paraphrase-19 ("Which method solves every quadratic equation, even one that I cannot factor?", page 15, the quadratic formula): the returned chunks were from section 2.5, pages 9 to 14 (factoring, square roots, completing the square), which also talk about solving quadratics. The words "every" and "cannot factor" point to the methods that come before. With the BM25 weight 0.5 the dense ranking brings page 15 into the top 8. The dense list alone still misses two questions at Recall@8 (0.9737), so the fusion helps.

All three had correct labels. They were real retrieval misses, mostly ranking inside one dense neighborhood.
