# Retrieval evaluation (phase 4)

Retrieval quality decides answer quality. This phase measures it with fixed data, protects it in CI, and gives the numbers that tune the chunker, the fusion, and the relevance gate.

## What we measure

- **Recall@1**: the share of questions where the first source is relevant. It is in the benchmarks summary and in the report, and a drop of more than 0.01 fails the check like the other recalls.
- **Recall@5 and Recall@8**: the share of questions where at least one relevant chunk is in the top 5 or the top 8 fused results. The chat prompt uses the top 8.
- **nDCG@8**: ranking quality of the list that the API returns, with graded relevance (2 for the labeled page, 1 for an adjacent page). Recall, MRR, and nDCG all read that list.
- **MRR**: the mean of 1 / rank of the first relevant chunk.
- **Gate accuracy**: questions that the course does not cover must be gated (no sources), and covered questions must not be. There are two kinds of uncovered questions: `not_covered` (nearby math that the excerpt does not teach, the hard negatives) and `off_topic` (no relation to the course: general knowledge, other subjects, small talk). Report the false-gate rate (covered questions that are cut off) and the missed-gate rate (uncovered questions that get sources) separately, and the missed-gate rate for `not_covered`, for `off_topic`, and for both together.
- **Gate inputs**: for every question the report holds what the gate reads: the best dense distance, the top BM25 score, and the lexical term coverage (the share of the distinct analyzed query terms that the best BM25 hit holds, and the number of query terms that exist in the index). A sweep over candidate gate rules and thresholds is computed from them.
- **Latency** (live mode only): p50 and p95 for dense, lexical, and the whole retrieval step.

A chunk is relevant when its page range includes a labeled page. Every metric is reported for dense only, BM25 only, and hybrid.

## Data

- `evals/corpora/<id>/`: one corpus. The evaluation has several, and every command runs all of them (use `--corpus <id>` for one). A corpus holds one PDF (an excerpt of about 40 pages of an openly licensed OpenStax book, CC BY 4.0), an `ATTRIBUTION.md` that names the source, the licence, and the changes (page selection only), `sections.json`, `golden.jsonl`, `cache/`, and `cache-dev/`. The two corpora are `college-algebra` (mathematics: quadratics, functions, and exponents, with worked examples, exercises, figures, and display math) and `american-government` (prose: the origins of the Constitution and American federalism, with headings, figures with captions, tables, a key terms list, and a summary). A corpus that is not mathematics has no `notation` questions.
- `evals/corpora/<id>/golden.jsonl`: one question per line: `{ "id", "question", "relevantPages": [n], "kind" }`. `kind` is one of `lookup` (close to the book's words), `paraphrase` (different words), `notation` (written in math notation, for example `x^2 - 5x + 6 = 0`), `followup` (needs the previous question, given in `context`), and `not_covered` (a nearby math topic that the excerpt does not teach), and `off_topic` (no relation to the course). A question is `not_covered` only when no page teaches the answer: a mention in a chapter outline or in a list of learning objectives does not count as teaching. At least 60 questions in a corpus, and at least 10 of each kind except `notation`, which a math corpus needs and a prose corpus does not. Draft questions may come from a model, but every question is checked by hand against the page before it goes in.
- `evals/corpora/<id>/sections.json`: splits the excerpt into sections. The evaluation treats each section as one document, so the per-document cap works as it does in a course with several PDFs. The `--cap-off` mode gives every chunk the same document ID, so the cap sees one document and never skips a chunk. It changes only the document ID: the chunks keep the section boundaries and the section titles stay in the embedded text, so it is not a real one-PDF course. It isolates the effect of the cap. Every report shows both modes. The evaluation does not measure source diversity (how many documents the sources come from), which is what the cap is for.
- `evals/corpora/<id>/cache-dev/`: a development cache (PDF text layer and fake embeddings) that shows the harness works. It is never used for the baseline, and `--check` refuses it.
- `evals/corpora/<id>/cache/`: the inputs that make the offline mode deterministic: the page Markdown that real ingestion produced for the corpus (Claude transcription included), and the embeddings of every chunk and every question (keyed by embedding model and the SHA-256 of the exact embedded text, stored as compressed 16-bit floats).

## Benchmarks

`evals/benchmarks.json` is a small public summary of the evaluation, for the README and the landing page. `offline --write-baseline` writes it with the baseline, and `offline --check` fails when it is stale. It has a stable schema (`schema: 1`): the embedding model, the number of questions, for each corpus the id, title, licence, source URL on openstax.org, pages and questions (from `corpus.json` of the corpus), the retrieval metrics (Recall@1, Recall@5, Recall@8, nDCG@8, MRR) for hybrid, dense only, and BM25 only, and the gate metrics (accuracy, false-gate rate, and the share of `off_topic` and of `not_covered` questions that the gate caught), each for all corpora pooled and for each corpus. The README table between `<!-- benchmarks:start -->` and `<!-- benchmarks:end -->` is generated from it (`make benchmarks`, and `make eval` checks it), and the badges of the README read the file on the main branch. The questions are the ones that the retrieval was tuned on, so the numbers are in-sample. The pooled numbers weigh every question the same, not every corpus, so the larger corpus counts more (college-algebra has 107 of 180 questions, about 59 percent).

## Modes

- **Offline** (`make eval`): chunk the cached pages with the real chunker, build the real BM25 index and an in-memory dense index from the cached embeddings, run the real retrieval and fusion code for every golden question, and print the metrics table. No AWS, no network, deterministic, a few seconds. A missing cache entry is an error, not a silent skip.
- **Grid** (`make eval-grid`): runs every combination of the tuning knobs (see "Tuning") offline and writes a table to `target/eval/grid.md` and `grid.json`. No AWS.
- **Live** (`make eval-live` with `AWS_PROFILE` and `AWS_REGION=ap-southeast-2`): ingest the corpus PDF through the real worker code with real Bedrock transcription and Titan embeddings, query real S3 Vectors in the dev stack, measure latency, and refresh the `cache/` of each corpus (the diff is reviewed and committed like code). The first version of the refresh runs the pipeline on this machine and does not query S3 Vectors: it records the time of the Titan query embedding calls, and the S3 Vectors and BM25 latency measurement is still to do. The cache holds the chunk embeddings of every chunk target of the grid (300, 400, and 600 tokens).
- **Incremental live refresh** (`make eval-live-missing`): fills what the caches of all corpora lack, in one plan. A corpus that has a cache reuses its pages, does no transcription, and gets Bedrock calls only for the embeddings and the follow-up rewrites that its cache does not have (new or changed questions, chunks of a new chunk target, and chunks that changed because the chunker changed). A corpus that has no cache yet gets the full live refresh with the models of the other caches: pdfium, page transcription only for the pages with a poor text layer (as the worker does), the follow-up rewrites, and all embeddings. The command prints the number of calls of each kind and an upper bound of the cost first, and starts only with `YES=1`. Both live commands follow `AWS_REGION`: set it to `ap-southeast-2`.

## Protection in CI

`evals/baseline.json` holds the offline metrics of the committed code. The `checks` workflow runs `make eval` and fails when any hybrid metric drops by more than 0.01 (absolute) below the baseline, or when gate accuracy drops at all, or when the false-gate rate rises at all. It checks the run with the cap and the cap-off run, both with the same tolerance. `--check` cannot be combined with `--write-baseline`. A change that improves the metrics updates the baseline in the same commit.

## Tuning

`openbook-eval grid` runs the whole grid offline: RRF `k` 30, 60, and 100; final sources 6, 8, and 10; candidates per retriever 20 and 40; most sources per document 2, 3, 4, 6, and 8 (8 is the same as no cap); the weight of the BM25 list in the fusion 0.5, 0.75, 1.0, and 1.5; the chunk target 300, 400, and 600 tokens; and the removal of running headers (`off`: production, no removal; `keep-first` and `drop-all`: the experimental word rule, which fails on short pages, see `docs/retrieval.md`). Each setting runs with the cap and with the cap off, for each corpus and for all corpora pooled. The whole grid is a tool (`make eval-grid`); the tests run `grid --tiny`, which has three rows. `offline` takes the same knobs as flags (`--rrf-k`, `--final-sources`, `--dense-candidates`, `--lexical-candidates`, `--max-per-document`, `--lexical-weight`, `--chunk-tokens`, `--header-variant keep-first|drop-all`, `--cap-off`) and the gate flags (`--gate any|dense|combined`, `--gate-distance`, `--gate-coverage`, `--gate-matched`) for one run. The production defaults stay as constants in `openbook-search`. The API never reads tuning flags.

Change a default only when the pooled hybrid Recall@8 or nDCG@8 improves without hurting gate accuracy or the false-gate rate on either corpus. A change that passes this on paper still has to be more than one question of Recall@8 or 0.01 of nDCG@8 on both corpora, and it must not break a design limit (a chunk target of 600 tokens is the hard limit of the chunker). Results go to `evals/results/<date>.md` (round 1: 2026-11-17, round 2: 2026-11-18): one table per experiment, the chosen row marked, and short notes. Contextual retrieval with a short model-written context per chunk is a later experiment against this same harness.

### The gate

The gate decides whether a question has sources at all. A gate rule is data that the evaluation can sweep, not a branch in the API. The candidates are the dense distance only, and the dense distance or a strong lexical match (high term coverage and a minimum number of matched terms) for queries that embed badly, such as notation. The rule must have no false gate on the golden set, with a margin of at least 0.02 in distance beyond the worst covered question. Among the rules that pass, the one with the highest gate accuracy wins, and when two rules differ by one question or less, the simpler one wins. The golden sets have 180 questions (107 and 73), so the rule does not fit many parameters. Round 1 chose the dense distance only, limit 0.78 for Titan v2 (`evals/results/2026-11-17.md`). Round 2 re-measured it on a second corpus in a prose subject and kept it (`evals/results/2026-11-18.md`). The worst covered question of the 123 is 0.758 (a prose paraphrase), so 0.78 has a margin of 0.022, and it has the best gate accuracy of the limits that pass (0.8944); 0.79 has a margin of 0.032 and 0.8833, and is the next step if real distances show that the margin is thin. The margin is a safety condition and it comes before the gate accuracy: a false gate (a student with a real course question gets no sources) costs more than a missed gate (the question gets sources that the answer may not cite, and the UI shows only the cited ones). The false-gate rate is 0 on both corpora, and all 24 `off_topic` questions are still gated (the nearest is at 0.82). "No false gate" is in-sample: the limit was chosen on the golden questions that the retrieval was tuned on. The API stores the real distances in `trace.gate` for the next round. The BM25 weight of the fusion must stay at 1.0 or more: a weight below about 0.9 would let BM25 only re-order dense results, never add a source (with k 60 and 40 dense candidates). Round 2 tested 0.5 and 0.75 again on a second corpus: Recall@1 and MRR rise on both corpora, but Recall@8 does not move, and the corpora are too small to show what the property costs, so it stays until a larger corpus says otherwise.

The distance limit belongs to one embedding model. Titan v2 has its own rule. The fake embedder (hashed bag of words, local runs and tests) and any model that has not been measured keep the first rule (any BM25 hit passes), because their distances have a different scale. `GateRule::for_embedding_model` picks the rule from the model ID of the embedder, so a Titan limit never gates the fake model.

The retrieval code that the API uses (query cleaning, fusion, the per-document cap, the gate) lives in `openbook-search`, so the evaluation runs exactly the production code.
